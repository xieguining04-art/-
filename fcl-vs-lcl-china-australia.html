One of the most common questions from Australian importers is whether they should use FCL (Full Container Load) or LCL (Less than Container Load) when buying from China.

The answer is not simply “LCL for small cargo and FCL for large cargo.” The most economical choice depends on the total logistics cost from the Chinese supplier to the Australian destination, including consolidation, origin handling, ocean freight, destination charges, customs clearance and delivery.

1. FCL vs LCL: what is the difference?

FCL — Full Container Load

With FCL, one shipper books the whole container. The cargo is loaded at a factory, warehouse or container yard in China and the container normally stays sealed until destination handling requires it to be opened.

Common equipment includes 20GP, 40GP and 40HQ.

LCL — Less than Container Load

With LCL, cargo from several shippers is consolidated into the same container. Your goods are delivered to a consolidation warehouse in China, loaded with other shipments, then deconsolidated at the destination warehouse in Australia.

2. Which is cheaper: FCL or LCL?

For small shipments, LCL is usually cheaper because the importer only pays for the space used instead of booking an entire container.

As cargo volume grows, the calculation changes. LCL costs often increase with each CBM while many FCL charges are container-based. At some point, the total LCL cost can approach or exceed the cost of a 20GP.

Compare:
LCL ocean freight + origin CFS/warehouse + destination deconsolidation + delivery
versus
FCL ocean freight + container/local charges + container delivery

This is why the cheapest ocean freight line on a quotation does not necessarily produce the cheapest shipment.

3. At what CBM does FCL become cheaper than LCL?

There is no fixed CBM threshold that works for every China–Australia shipment.

As a practical planning rule, once cargo reaches around 12–15 CBM, many importers should ask for both an LCL quote and a 20GP FCL quote. The actual break-even point can be lower or higher depending on:

  • Cargo weight and density
  • Chinese origin city and consolidation warehouse
  • Australian port
  • LCL minimum charges
  • Destination unpack and warehouse fees
  • Current 20GP container rate
  • Final delivery postcode
  • Special handling requirements
Do not use 12–15 CBM as an automatic booking rule. Use it as the point where you start comparing both complete quotations.

4. Australian destination charges can change the answer

Destination charges are especially important when comparing LCL and FCL.

LCL may involve charges for deconsolidation, warehouse handling, release documentation and delivery from the destination CFS. FCL may involve shipping-line local charges, container release, terminal/depot arrangements and container delivery.

Always compare the total destination cost, not only the China-side freight.

5. FCL vs LCL transit time

The vessel transit itself may be similar if both shipments use the same sailing, but the total door-to-door time can differ.

LCL normally requires additional time for:

  • Delivery into the consolidation warehouse
  • Waiting for container consolidation
  • Destination deconsolidation
  • Warehouse release

FCL can therefore be operationally faster and more predictable for larger shipments, although actual timing depends on carrier schedules, customs and biosecurity.

6. Cargo handling, damage risk and control

LCL cargo is handled more frequently because it must be received, consolidated, unloaded and separated from other shipments. Good packaging is therefore particularly important.

FCL generally offers more control over loading and cargo securing. This can be valuable for:

  • Furniture and cabinets
  • Fragile products
  • Machinery
  • Oversized cartons
  • High-value commercial cargo

FCL does not eliminate cargo risk. Correct blocking, bracing, weight distribution and moisture protection are still essential.

7. 20GP vs 40HQ for FCL shipping

A 20GP is often suitable for dense or heavy cargo, while a 40HQ is commonly chosen for larger-volume cargo.

ContainerTypical useKey consideration
20GPHeavy machinery, dense building materials, smaller full loadsWeight may become the limit before volume
40GPGeneral full-container cargoLower internal height than 40HQ
40HQFurniture, cabinets, packaging, high-volume mixed goodsBetter cubic capacity for lighter/voluminous cargo

Actual loading capacity depends on packaging dimensions and loading method. A theoretical CBM figure is not the same as usable loading capacity.

8. Which option is better for different cargo types?

Furniture & cabinets

FCL often becomes attractive because the cargo is bulky, and fewer handling points can reduce damage risk.

Building materials

Dense cargo may suit a 20GP. Weight distribution and container payload need to be checked carefully.

Small commercial orders

LCL is often the practical choice when volume is low and the importer wants to avoid paying for unused container space.

Machinery & sensitive equipment

FCL may provide better loading control, but dimensions, centre of gravity and securing requirements must be assessed.

9. Can multiple Chinese suppliers share one FCL container?

Yes. Multiple suppliers can send goods to a China consolidation warehouse before loading one 20GP or 40HQ.

A consolidation service can include:

  • Supplier pickup or warehouse receiving
  • Carton counting and basic condition checking
  • Labelling and sorting
  • Document coordination
  • Loading plan preparation
  • Container loading

This can be more efficient than shipping several separate LCL consignments when the combined volume becomes large enough.

12. Frequently asked questions

Is FCL or LCL cheaper from China to Australia?

LCL is usually more economical for smaller shipments, while FCL can become more cost-effective as volume increases. The correct choice depends on total origin, ocean freight, destination handling and delivery costs rather than ocean freight alone.

At what CBM should I compare FCL and LCL?

There is no universal break-even point. As a practical rule, once cargo reaches roughly 12–15 CBM, many importers should compare a 20GP FCL option against LCL. The actual break-even point changes by route, weight, cargo type and destination charges.

Is FCL faster than LCL from China to Australia?

FCL is often operationally simpler at origin and destination because the cargo does not need consolidation and deconsolidation. LCL may require extra warehouse handling, so total door-to-door time can be longer even when the vessel transit is similar.

Which is safer for fragile or high-value cargo, FCL or LCL?

FCL usually gives the importer more control because the cargo is handled fewer times and does not share container space with unrelated shipments. Correct packing and securing are still essential.

Can I consolidate cargo from several Chinese suppliers into one FCL container?

Yes. Multiple suppliers can deliver cargo to a China warehouse for checking, consolidation and container loading before export, provided the documentation and loading plan are coordinated correctly.

Should I choose 20GP or 40HQ for FCL shipping to Australia?

A 20GP is often suitable for dense or heavy cargo, while a 40HQ is commonly used for higher-volume cargo such as furniture, cabinets and mixed consumer goods. The best choice depends on cargo dimensions, weight and loading efficiency.

Final recommendation

Use LCL when the shipment is genuinely small. As volume grows, compare the complete LCL landed logistics cost against a 20GP or 40HQ FCL solution. The best answer is based on current rates, destination charges, cargo handling and total delivery cost—not one fixed CBM rule.
